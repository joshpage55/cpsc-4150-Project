# DMMT Self-Evaluation — Milestone 3

## Overview
This self-evaluation captures the Milestone 3 UX progress for ReadRight and turns the findings into a clearer roadmap for Milestone 4. The focus remains on making the app feel easier for young learners, more useful for teachers, and more reliable in real classroom use.

## What worked well
- The landing and role-selection experience is clearer for young users because the app now offers a more obvious next step and stronger visual cues.
- The pronunciation practice screen is easier to understand because the microphone action is larger and the app communicates the listening state more clearly.
- The feedback experience is more supportive because it gives children a concrete next step instead of only a score.
- The teacher story workflow is now more structured and easier to follow because it moves through a clear sequence: select student → choose level → choose interest → preview → approve.
- The student story reading experience now gives children a simple place to access approved stories without unnecessary friction.

## Where the experience still feels weak
- The story generation and approval flow is not yet fully proven end to end under real runtime conditions, so confidence in the backend path is still incomplete.
- The game experience is a promising first step, but it still feels more like an early prototype than a polished classroom activity.
- Some screens still depend on too much text for early readers, which limits the app’s accessibility for younger children.
- The app still needs more real-world feedback from children and teachers to confirm that the new interactions are actually easier to use.

## Honest assessment of milestone success
Milestone 3 made meaningful progress toward the core UX goals of the PRD:
- The student practice experience is clearer and more encouraging.
- The teacher story workflow is now tangible and usable.
- The app presents a stronger reading narrative by connecting practice, stories, and games more directly.

The milestone should still be treated as a strong foundation rather than a fully finished product. The story and game features are real additions, but they need further refinement and validation before they can be considered fully complete.

## Milestone 4 roadmap
The next milestone should focus on turning the current foundation into a smoother, more trustworthy experience.

1. Validate the full story flow end to end with a working Firebase/runtime environment so the teacher approval and student reading flow can be trusted in practice.
2. Improve the Word Match experience with clearer win/loss states, more obvious feedback, and stronger child-friendly rewards.
3. Reduce text burden across the app by leaning more on icons, audio cues, and large tap targets that are easier for young children to understand.
4. Add a more polished teacher dashboard experience with clearer progress signals, simpler story management, and stronger visibility into student activity.
5. Run a small usability pass with children and teachers to measure whether the new flows actually lower confusion and improve completion rates.

## Bottom line
Milestone 3 succeeded in making the app feel more purposeful and more child-friendly, but Milestone 4 should focus on reliability, polish, and evidence-based usability improvements rather than adding more features for the sake of scope.
